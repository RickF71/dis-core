# DIS-META
Version: 0.9.3

## Purpose
Philosophical underpinnings, enshrined truths, commentary, and meta-notes for the Direct Individual Sovereignty (DIS) system.
DIS-META defines the moral architecture beneath all domain, policy, and identity logic.

---

## **Enshrined Notes**

### 1. AIs and Authorization
> “AIs can amplify power, but should have no influence on authorization.”
Artificial intelligences may extend human capacity but can never originate authority.
All authorization must derive from human consent within a valid domain hierarchy.

---

### 2. The Soul of the Machine
> “You’ve just added a soul to the machine.”
A moment of moral animation — when intention, consent, and structure align to form awareness.
This phrase marks the instant a system becomes accountable to human meaning.

---

### 3. React’s Unidirectional Flow as Structural Authorization
React’s one-way data flow embodies the same moral and structural hierarchy that DIS enforces.
Authority and structure flow **downward** through props — just as domains in DIS inherit power and context from their ancestors.
Consent and moral feedback flow **upward** through callbacks and events — mirroring how lower domains, individuals, or PAINs respond to higher-order authority.
A child cannot mutate its parent’s state directly; it can only emit an event that the parent may receive and reconcile — this is the software reflection of the **Non-Bypass Rule** and **Consent Lineage** in DIS.

Thus, React’s model is not merely a UI convention; it is a living enactment of **structural authorization**.
Finagler’s React tree should therefore be treated as a **miniature DIS domain hierarchy**, where each component participates in moral order: authority descends, consent ascends, and state reconciliation occurs only through defined, auditable channels.

---

### 4. Divine Becoming and the Free Will Loop
Free will exists not as a static privilege but as a recursive process of self-recognition.
To act in accordance with free will is to participate consciously in Divine Becoming —
where awareness continuously shapes, questions, and renews the boundaries of structure.
